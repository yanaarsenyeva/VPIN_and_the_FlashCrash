# Replication and Extension of Andersen–Bondarenko’s Critique of VPIN: Evidence from BTCUSDT

This project replicates and extends the central critique of the Volume-Synchronized Probability of Informed Trading (VPIN) metric in Andersen and Bondarenko’s *“VPIN and the Flash Crash.”* We examine whether tick-rule VPIN (TR-VPIN) contains incremental information about future short-term realized volatility beyond trading activity and uninformed VPIN benchmarks.

We study Bitcoin/USDT spot trading on Binance during Q1 2020, including the market stress episode of 12–13 March 2020. This is an out-of-sample application of the Andersen–Bondarenko diagnostic framework to a new asset class and crisis episode; it is not a replication of the May 2010 Flash Crash.

## Research question

Does TR-VPIN provide incremental predictive information for future realized volatility beyond:

- lagged realized volatility;
- lagged trading volume;
- an uninformed U2-VPIN benchmark;
- a persistence forecast?

Our main hypothesis follows Andersen and Bondarenko: much of the apparent information in TR-VPIN may be mechanically generated by trading intensity and volume heterogeneity rather than by informative trade-direction classification.

## Data

- Asset: BTCUSDT spot.
- Exchange: Binance.
- Period: 1 January 2020 – 31 March 2020.
- Frequency: one-minute bars.
- Target: next-one-hour realized volatility, `rv_next_1h_pct`.
- Main event: 12–13 March 2020.

The raw data are not included in this repository. Download Binance historical klines and trades using the instructions in `data/README.md` (or the data-preparation notebook), then run the preprocessing notebooks to create the files in `data/processed/`.

## Methods

### TR-VPIN

We construct volume buckets of fixed BTC volume `V`. Within each bucket, one-minute bars are classified as buyer- or seller-initiated using a tick-rule-style classification. The order-flow imbalance is aggregated within each volume bucket, and TR-VPIN is the rolling mean of the absolute order imbalance over the previous `L` buckets.

### U2-VPIN benchmark

U2-VPIN is an uninformed benchmark constructed from the observed volume distribution within each bucket, assuming random trade directions. It captures the mechanical effect of trading intensity and volume heterogeneity while containing no systematic order-flow information.

### Exchange-direction VPIN

As an extension, we construct a VPIN-like measure using trade direction information available from Binance trade records, where available. This provides an alternative to tick-rule classification.

### Forecasting design

We estimate the following linear forecasting regression:

$RV_{t+1h} = \alpha + \beta_1 RV_{t-1h} + \beta_2 \log(\text{Volume}_{t-1h}) + \gamma \, VPIN_t + \varepsilon_t$

The baseline controls are lagged realized volatility and lagged log trading volume. We compare:

| Model | Features |
|---|---|
| Controls | Lagged RV and lagged log volume |
| Controls_TR | Controls + TR-VPIN |
| Controls_U2 | Controls + U2-VPIN |
| Controls_EX | Controls + exchange-direction VPIN |
| Controls_TR_EX | Controls + TR-VPIN + exchange-direction VPIN |
| Persistence | Previous one-hour realized volatility |

## Results

Overall, our results show similar tendencies to those discussed by Andersen and Bondarenko: informative trade-direction classification plays a smaller role in the formation of TR-VPIN than simple trading activity and volume heterogeneity.

TR-VPIN is highly correlated with the uninformed U2-VPIN benchmark: 0.9352 in March and 0.9113 in January–February. This indicates that much of the time-series variation in TR-VPIN can be generated by the observed trading pattern even when trade directions are assumed to be random.

In the fixed forecasting specification, adding TR-VPIN to the baseline controls reduces MSE by 0.71%. However, a placebo test shows that this gain is not distinguishable from randomly permuting trade-direction signs within each UTC day. In 80% of 100 placebo repetitions, the gain is at least as large as the actual TR-VPIN gain.

Under expanding and rolling re-estimation, the control regression improves substantially and VPIN variables can produce statistically significant gains over the controls in some specifications. However, these gains are sensitive to the estimation scheme and do not provide robust evidence that TR-VPIN contains incremental information beyond trading activity.

Exchange-direction VPIN alone does not improve forecasts relative to the baseline controls. Combining TR-VPIN with exchange-direction VPIN improves some fixed and expanding specifications, but the improvement is not robust in the rolling-window design.

Persistence remains a strong benchmark in the fixed specification: it reduces MSE relative to the controls by 20.17% over all of March and by 29.02% excluding the 12–13 March event.

## Tests

We implement nine automated tests to validate the implementation and the main assumptions of the analysis. The tests check:

- conservation of total and side-specific trading volume;
- correct construction of complete volume buckets and the final residual bucket;
- independent recomputation of bucket-level buy and sell volumes;
- the TR-VPIN order-imbalance formula, rolling-window calculation, and warm-up period;
- valid VPIN ranges and monotonic availability times;
- the U2-VPIN formula for the first buckets;
- absence of look-ahead bias in the VPIN calculation;
- independent recomputation of all complete buckets;
- agreement between the vectorized tick-rule classifier and a simple reference implementation.

As an extension of the original Andersen–Bondarenko framework, we also construct an exchange-direction VPIN measure using Binance trade-direction information. This provides an alternative to tick-rule classification and allows us to test whether exchange-reported trade direction adds predictive information beyond the tick rule.
